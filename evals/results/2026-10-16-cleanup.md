# English cleanup: rules, then a small local model?

Date: 2026-10-16 (runs on 2026-10-15 and 2026-10-16 UTC). Branch: `feat/cleanup-rules-evals`, PR #48.
This is the round 2 write-up. Round 1's numbers are kept at the end, labelled in-sample and
superseded.

## Question

Deepgram formats, it doesn't clean up. The cleanup order is: vocabulary, deterministic rules
(fillers and stutters), a small local model, then the user's own rules. Is a local model worth
shipping on top of the rules, and which one?

The decision rule, fixed before the round 2 runs: ship the model only if rules + S1-mini + guard
loses no more spoken words than rules-only on **both** the in-sample and the held-out set, under
the spoken-words-lost metric below. Otherwise the recommendation is rules-only, with S1-mini
behind a hidden flag. Whether a shipped model would be a Settings toggle is a wiring decision,
not this one.

## Answer

**Ship rules-only. Keep S1-mini behind a hidden flag.**

Rules + S1-mini + guard lost spoken words on both sets, and rules-only lost none:

| Set | Rules only: words lost | Rules + S1-mini + guard: words lost | Rule met? |
|---|---|---|---|
| In-sample, 188 cases (Linux) | 0 | 4 (in 4 cases, all clean texts) | No |
| Held-out, 73 cases (Linux) | 0 | 4 (in 2 cases, both clean texts) | No |
| In-sample subset, 60 cases (macOS CPU) | 0 | 1 | No |
| Held-out, 73 cases (macOS CPU) | 0 | 1 | No |

Restraint, reported next to it: rules-only returned every clean text byte for byte (100% on both
sets). Rules + S1-mini + guard changed 7 of 88 clean in-sample texts (92.0%) and 3 of 38 clean
held-out texts (92.1%). What the model buys is edit accuracy: 86.0% against 45.0% in-sample and
77.1% against 31.4% held-out, almost all of it on self-corrections and spoken numbers, emails and
paths that the rules don't touch.

SpeakoFlow Mini fails the same rule: with the guard it lost 3 spoken words in-sample (0 held-out).

Nothing in this PR wires either the rules or a model into dictation. A later workstream does.

## Where the words went

Rules + S1-mini + guard, in-sample (all four are clean texts the guard let through):

- k09: "He said that that was the last time" became "He said that was the last time".
- k10: "She had had enough of the noise." became "She had enough of the noise."
- k08: "It was, like, really cold outside, you know?" lost "like".
- k24: "lol ok" became "Lol, okay." ("ok" to "okay" counts as one lost, one added).

Rules + S1-mini + guard, held-out (Linux):

- h34: "The word um shows up a lot in transcripts." lost "um", the word being talked about.
- h30: "We won 3 to 2 after extra time." became "We won 3-2 after extra time." The score is kept,
  but the metric reads "3-2" as one number (it joins adjacent numbers), so it counts 3 lost and

  is why macOS shows 1 lost word, not 4.

Rules + SpeakoFlow Mini + guard, in-sample: k08 lost "you know" (2 words) and k09 lost a "that".

Without the guard, S1-mini also dropped "no" from "No, no, I did not say that." (k11), dropped
"Uh-huh" (k15), rewrote "gonna" as "I'm going to" (k23) and split `useLayoutEffect` into "use

(k13), "Swap the order:" (h38) and "not not useLayoutEffect" (g11), shortened "three point two
million dollars" to "three million dollars" (n12) and turned "help dot example dot com" into
"example.com" (n08). The guard rejected each one.

## The guard: what it caught and what it missed

Of S1-mini's 188 in-sample outputs the guard rejected 131: 124 had exactly the input's words
(format-only edits, so the rules text carries the same words anyway), 4 dropped content, 2
dropped a negation and 1 changed a number. 112 of the 131 rejected outputs matched the expected
text, almost all clean or rules-fixed cases where the model echoed its input, so rejecting them
cost nothing. Held-out: 50 rejects (48 no word change, 1 negation, 1 changed number).
SpeakoFlow Mini: 146 in-sample and 57 held-out rejects.

Known gaps, found on these runs and **not fixed in this PR**:

1. **Function-word and filler deletions pass.** k08, k09, k10 and h34 all drop a word the guard
   doesn't count as content ("like", "that", "had", "um"). The guard's drop check works on
   content words, so a lost duplicate function word or a mentioned filler is invisible to it.
2. **"three oh four" vs "three and four".** SpeakoFlow Mini turned "room three oh four" into
   "room three and four" (h72) and the guard accepted it: its number parser doesn't read "oh" as
   zero and skips "and", so both sides parse as 3 and 4. That's a changed number getting through.
   The same blind spot rejected S1-mini's correct "room 304" for h72 as a changed number.
3. **False rejects.** S1-mini's "Do call the client today." for h62 is the right repair, but the
   guard rejected it as a dropped negation. Together with h72 above, these cost edit accuracy,
   not safety.

Gaps 1 and 2 showed up partly on the held-out set. Fixing them now would be tuning on it, so a
fix needs a fresh held-out set written first. Under the decision rule they change nothing: the
recommendation is already rules-only.

## The 55% claim: not comparable

SpeakoFlow's model card lists S1-mini at 55.3% content damage (15.3% overall, 22.1% restraint,
8.5% edit accuracy). Our numbers can't confirm or refute that, because the setups differ in ways
that matter:

- SpeakoFlow's card says: "**Every system was given the same short system prompt, with reasoning
  off.**" That is SpeakoFlow's own prompt, not the system prompt and control line the S1-mini card
  says the model needs. We ran S1-mini with its own prompt and control line.
- The S1-mini card says the model "takes a raw ASR transcript and rewrites it as clean written
  text", in a pipeline of audio, then ASR (Whisper, Parakeet and so on), then S1-mini. We feed it
  Deepgram smart-formatted text that our rules have already cleaned.
- SpeakoFlow's cases and metric are private.

For the record, under our metric and setup, S1-mini with no guard lost spoken words on 4.3% of
in-sample cases and 2.7% of held-out cases.

## The metric: spoken words lost

`run_eval.py` turns the input, the output and each reference (the expected text and every
accepted alternative) into a multiset of every spoken token: function words and negations
included, contractions expanded ("don't" counts as "do not"), spoken numbers turned into digit
strings ("forty two" and "42" count the same) and adjacent numbers joined. For each reference,
a word is lost when the reference keeps it (up to the number of times the speaker said it) and
the output has fewer copies. The case's score is the smallest loss over the references, so a
correction the reference expects is never counted. It's written apart from the guard on purpose,
so it doesn't share the guard's blind spots.

Round 1 used a different metric: "a distinct content word of the expected text is missing".
It ignored function words, negations, duplicates and order, and counted every number as one
item. That's why round 1 reported 0.0% loss for S1-mini + guard while the same kind of output
("She had had enough" to "She had enough", "No, no" to "No") loses words under the new metric.
It also produced false alarms: on the held-out set it flags rules-only for h71, whose output keeps
every spoken word ("two hundred milligrams") but lacks the reference's "mg". Both metrics are in
every raw file (`content_loss_v1` is the old one).

## Round 1 (superseded, in-sample)

Round 1 ran the first rules and guard on the 188 in-sample cases and scored them with the old
metric. Its headline: rules + S1-mini + guard at 90.9% restraint, 94.0% edit accuracy and 0.0%
content loss, against rules-only at 100.0%, 52.0% and 0.0%; SpeakoFlow Mini + guard at 95.5%,
87.0% and 1.1%. On that basis it recommended S1-mini as an opt-in download. Those numbers are
in-sample (the guard and two accept lists were changed after reading them, and the "dev/test"
split wasn't held out), the old metric missed lost function words and negations, and round 1's
rules collapsed some number words and repeated content words. That recommendation is withdrawn.
Round 1's ablations (all in-sample, old metric) still stand as observations: `semi-casual`
styling lowercases the first word and drops the final full stop, so the control line uses
`semi-formal` unless the user's style says otherwise; `Context: email` made no measurable
difference behind the guard; and running the rules first added 2 to 3 points of edit accuracy
for both models.

## Conclusion and what changed

- Recommendation: rules-only. S1-mini (Q4_K_M, revision above) stays behind a hidden flag with
  `CleanupGuard` in front of it, the rules text on any reject, timeout or error, `-c 2048`, and
  credited as "S1-mini" by "Superwhisper". No opt-in download.
- `CleanupRules`, `CleanupGuard` and `CleanupControlLine` ship in the core (Swift and TypeScript),
  not wired into dictation yet. The rules are safe to wire in: 100% restraint and no lost words
  on both sets.
- SpeakoFlow Mini isn't recommended either: it lost words in-sample even behind the guard.
- Before anyone revisits the model: fix the guard's function-word blind spot and the "oh" digit,
  then write a fresh held-out set before running anything on the fix.
